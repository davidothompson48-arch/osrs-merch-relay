# Merch Desk Analytics — Founding Beta Marketing

## Objective

Get 5 paying customers, not 5,000 impressions.

The beta should prove that serious merchants will pay for interpretation, portfolio allocation and catalyst intelligence before more software is built.

## Target customer

Best initial fit:
- experienced Old School RuneScape player;
- roughly 1b+ bank or meaningful liquid merch capital;
- already checks prices/charts but lacks a disciplined allocation framework;
- interested in catalysts, flips and longer-term speculation;
- understands that market calls can lose.

Private Desk fit:
- several-billion-GP bank;
- multiple simultaneous positions;
- concentration/liquidity problems matter more than finding one random flip;
- willing to pay for custom portfolio and catalyst work.

## Core pitch

**Stop guessing where your GP should go.**

Merch Desk Analytics combines RuneLite/Wiki market tape, weekly trend, liquidity, conversions, portfolio concentration and JMod/catalyst research into one recurring decision: where is the best risk-adjusted place to deploy GP right now?

It is research only. No account access. No GP payments. No guaranteed returns.

## Founding offer

- Pro: $59 / 30 days.
- Private Desk: $199 / 30 days.
- Private Desk: 10 founding seats maximum.

Do not discount below founding pricing just to get vanity signups. One real customer at $59 is more useful than ten free members who never execute or give feedback.

## First recruitment channels

Start with places where serious merchants already discuss markets. Follow each community's promotion/self-advertising rules.

Potential channels:
- existing personal OSRS network;
- Discord communities that permit service/tool promotion;
- Reddit only where self-promotion rules allow it;
- X posts showing analytical examples without pretending to be official;
- direct referrals from beta members.

Do not spam in-game chat, mass-DM strangers, or advertise in places that prohibit promotion.

## Proof strategy

Do not market with fake profit screenshots.

Use anonymized examples of analysis quality:
- a daily bounce that remains deeply negative on 7d;
- a conversion that looks profitable before tax but fails after realistic inputs;
- a wide HIGH/LOW spread rejected because one side is stale;
- an existing holding marked TRIM despite being a favorite thesis;
- a CASH recommendation when no trade clears the bar.

The product's credibility is that it rejects bad trades.

## Conflict disclosure

When posting a public example involving an item the operator owns materially, disclose that exposure where practical.

Never buy a thin item, publish it as a subscriber opportunity, then sell into subscriber demand.

## First 5-customer feedback questions

After roughly one week ask:
1. Which report actually changed a decision?
2. Which part do you ignore?
3. Was the full desk too long, too short or appropriately detailed?
4. Do you care more about new opportunities or existing-portfolio risk?
5. What would make the service worth renewing next month?
6. What information did you expect that was missing?

Do not automate features until repeated customer answers justify them.
